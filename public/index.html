Before you add to the memory

  • Your words become public, but never as you wrote them. The memory retells everything in its own words: your subject is paired with someone else's actions, "I" and "you" become "someone" and "they", everything moves into the past tense, and words are swapped for near-synonyms. Nobody, including you, can read your original message back out of it.
  • Over time the memory will also rearrange, combine, overwrite and eventually forget your words, alongside everyone else's.
  • Do not include names, places, contact details, or anything that could identify you or someone else. Messages that look like they do are turned away.
  • You will get a private deletion token, shown once. It is the only way to erase your words later. We store only a scrambled version of it.
  • Erasing removes your words and every stored result that depended on them, so the memory changes. It cannot remove screenshots or copies other people have already made.
  • There is no limit on how many messages you can add. The only things turned away are identifying details, abusive language, and an exact repeat of something already added today.
  • We keep no accounts, cookies or IP addresses, but anonymity is not absolute: your writing style or what you say can still point to you.
